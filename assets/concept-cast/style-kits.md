# BookVizion House Style Kits — v6 working set (drafted 2026-10-18)

Working style names (Jon, 2026-10-18; renameable before launch): Watercolor / Polished Cartoon / Classic Storybook / Bold Comic / 3D Animated / Semi-Real Painterly.

**Polished Cartoon needs no new kit.** It is the proven house cast finish — Harlow's book was produced in it (polished storybook cast finish: dimensional cartoon lighting, clear light source, rounded form shading, glossy catchlights), and the Maya + Biscuit reference portraits are rendered in it. It is the control style every other kit is judged against.

How kits are used: a render attaches the character reference portrait(s) + the character recipe NEVER CHANGE facts + the scene recipe (for Set A) + exactly ONE kit's master style block below. The kit restyles the MEDIUM only — faces, markings, wardrobe, composition, and lighting direction never change. Every render is a CONCEPT SAMPLE in use. One likeness test per kit runs before any final (render order in PREP-PACK-INDEX.md).

---

## Kit A — Watercolor

**Master style block (attach verbatim):**
Soft transparent watercolor illustration, traditional storybook wash technique. Layered translucent washes with visible pigment pooling at the edges, gentle granulation, and real paper grain showing through the lightest passages. Colors bloom softly into one another; edges are painted, never outlined — forms are defined by value and hue shifts, not line. Faces keep the reference likeness with softly modeled cheeks and simple warm shadows. Light feels diffused and luminous, as if the scene glows from within the paper. Matte, quiet, tender finish. Never digital-flat, never photorealistic.

**Negatives:** No ink outlines or hard contour lines. No flat vector color, no cel shading, no glossy 3D surface, no airbrush smoothness, no photographic detail or skin texture. No neon or saturated digital color. No dark moody washes — keep the value range light and airy. No visible text, logos, or watermark. No lens blur, no bokeh, no depth of field.

**Palette notes:** Warm ivory paper base. Pigments stay in the warm storybook family — ochre, warm rose, sage, dusty blue — mixed on the paper, never pure tube brights. Dusk scenes deepen with blue-green washes laid over the warm ground, letting the warm underpainting glow through on the subjects.

**Finish rules:** Paper grain visible across the whole image, strongest in skies and light passages. At least some edges left as lost-and-found pigment edges (no edge may be a drawn line). Character identity anchors (hair puffs, gap tooth, collar and tag, white chest) must survive the softness — if an anchor can't be read, the render fails the kit.

## Kit B — Bold Comic

**Master style block (attach verbatim):**
Bold comic illustration with clean confident ink outlines and flat cel color. Every form is closed with a smooth dark ink contour of even weight, filled with one flat color plus a single shade step — no gradients, no blended shading. Colors are bright, clear, and high-contrast; compositions read instantly at thumbnail size. Faces keep the reference likeness in simplified comic features with expressive, readable expressions. Energy is playful and punchy, like a modern all-ages graphic novel. Halftone-free: no dot patterns anywhere. Never photorealistic, never painterly.

**Negatives:** No halftone dots, no Ben-Day patterns, no screentones. No painterly blending, no watercolor bleed, no paper grain texture, no soft gradients or airbrushing. No scratchy or sketchy line work, no wobbly contours. No speech balloons, captions, sound-effect lettering, panel borders, text, logos, or watermark. No dark gritty or superhero-violent mood. No lens blur, no bokeh, no depth of field.

**Palette notes:** Flat primaries-leaning storybook palette: clear sky blues, warm reds, sunny yellows — Maya's mustard coat reads slightly deeper so it doesn't merge with flat yellow fills. One shade step per color, always a clean hue shift, never gray. Backgrounds stay one step quieter than the subjects so the inked figures pop.

**Finish rules:** Line weight even and confident; every shape closed. Exactly one shade step per fill — a second shade step is drift. Identity anchors drawn crisply (hair ties, collar tag, tail rings are inked shapes, not suggestions). The dusk scene keeps its composition and warm/cool contrast, translated into flat color: deep blue-green flat sky, warm flat light shapes on the subjects.

## Kit C — 3D Animated

**Master style block (attach verbatim):**
Soft 3D animated-film render in the modern family-animation style. Rounded, appealing forms with gentle stylization — never realistic anatomy. Skin and fur have a warm subsurface glow, as if light passes softly through them; fur is sculpted in soft grouped clumps, never individual strands. Eyes are large, glossy, and expressive with bright catchlights. Lighting is cinematic and soft: a warm key with cool ambient fill, gentle rim light separating the subjects from the background. Surfaces are matte and velvety, toy-like in their softness. This is an animated film frame, NEVER a photograph and never photoreal.

**Negatives:** No photorealism, no photographic skin, no pores, no real-hair simulation, no individual fur strands. No uncanny-valley faces, no realistic proportions, no live-action look. No hard plastic or glossy vinyl surfaces, no video-game render look, no sharp ray-traced reflections. No recognizable studio character design or house look — no existing animation studio's faces, proportions, or styling. No text, logos, or watermark. No lens blur, no bokeh, no depth of field.

**Palette notes:** Warm key against cool ambient, the strongest warm/cool split of the six kits — the dusk scene is this kit's home turf. Palette stays in the warm storybook family pushed slightly cinematic: amber and honey on the subjects, deep teal-blue-green in the sky and shadows. Saturation moderate; never neon, never HDR-crunchy.

**Finish rules:** Every surface passes the touch test — it should look soft enough to hug. Fur and hair read as sculpted masses with painted-in detail, not simulation. The likeness test for this kit runs FIRST among the style tests (with Semi-Real Painterly) per Ravyn A3, because stylization drift is likeliest here: face shape, puff shape, and the cat's markings must match the reference portraits exactly before the kit is approved.

## Kit D — Semi-Real Painterly

**Master style block (attach verbatim):**
Painterly realism in visible oil-and-gouache brushwork. Realistic proportions and anatomy softened by an illustrator's hand — true-to-life structure, but every surface is built from discernible brushstrokes, palette-knife edges, and broken color. Light is observed and naturalistic, modeled in warm and cool paint rather than rendered by a camera. Faces keep the reference likeness with lifelike warmth, gently idealized — a painted portrait, NEVER a photograph. No photographic skin texture, no pores, no camera detail; if it could be mistaken for a photo at arm's length, it has failed.

**Negatives:** No photography, no photoreal skin texture, no pores, no individual hairs, no lens effects (no depth-of-field blur, bokeh, chromatic aberration, or film grain). No smooth digital airbrush finish, no 3D render look. No hyperdetail. No text, logos, or watermark. No lens blur, no bokeh, no depth of field.

**Palette notes:** Naturalistic warm palette with broken color — warm ochres, umbers, and rose in the lit passages, blue-green and violet-gray in the dusk shadows, mixed optically by adjacent strokes rather than blended smooth. The most muted saturation of the six kits; richness comes from value contrast, not brightness.

**Finish rules:** Brushwork visible at reading distance in every passage — sky, skin, fur, and wood alike; a smooth passage is drift. Proportions are the closest to life of the six kits, but the cartoon identity anchors (puff shape, gap tooth, collar and tag, tail rings) are painted as designed, never "corrected" toward realism. The likeness test for this kit runs FIRST among the style tests (with 3D Animated) per Ravyn A3, because photo-drift is this kit's known failure mode; no photoreal finish passes, per Folio's sample limit.

## Kit E — Classic Storybook (NON-CHRISTMAS rebuild)

**This kit is a fresh build.** The earlier Santa book art was painted in a Christmas-specific gouache treatment; that treatment does NOT count as this kit and must not be attached or referenced for v6 samples. This kit is season-neutral: no Christmas or holiday elements of any kind appear in it, its palette, or its renders — no snow, no ornaments, no wreaths, no string lights shaped like holiday bulbs, no red-and-green holiday pairing, no winter clothing cues.

**Master style block (attach verbatim):**
Classic painted-gouache storybook illustration in the warm mid-century picture-book tradition. Opaque gouache laid in textured brush edges — paint sits ON the paper with visible dry-brush drag and slightly rough, hand-painted contours. Forms are simplified with an illustrator's economy: clear silhouettes, honest proportions gently softened, faces warm and expressive with minimal detail. Color is rich and matte, printed-book warm, with the limited-palette harmony of vintage four-color storybook printing. Cozy, timeless, hand-made finish. Never digital-smooth, never photorealistic.

**Negatives:** No Christmas or holiday elements — no snow, ornaments, wreaths, holly, stockings, Santa or elf figures, holiday bulb strings, candy canes, gift-wrap patterns, or winter/holiday clothing. No watercolor transparency (this is opaque gouache), no flat vector color, no ink outlines, no 3D surfaces, no photographic detail. No neon or modern saturated color. No text, logos, or watermark. No lens blur, no bokeh, no depth of field.

**Palette notes:** Warm mid-century storybook palette: brick red, mustard ochre, sage and moss greens, dusty teal, warm cream paper, umber shadows. Colors feel printed, slightly ink-muted, never screen-bright. For the dusk scene, the sky goes dusty deep teal with cream-warm light on the subjects — the string lights are plain warm round bulbs on a wire, backyard-fair style, reading as garden lighting only, never holiday decoration.

**Finish rules:** Gouache texture and dry-brush edges visible throughout; contours are painted, not inked. Silhouettes must read clearly at thumbnail size. Identity anchors stay exact under the simplification. Any render that drifts toward a holiday mood — through palette, props, or lighting ornaments — fails this kit outright, whatever its other merits.
